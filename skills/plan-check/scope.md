# Scope: where your plan lands

Like every scope file, this one says where the skill works: the rubric
(`rubric.md`) decides whether a plan package or your own plan draft is
READY; the scope decides which issues the skill may plan against at all,
and what the rules are where that plan lands.

## Where your issue lives

Any public repository, one issue at a time: the issue your claim
names. That issue comes to you through your own evidence trail: an
issue graded by issue-scout or issue-select, and the claim you posted on
it. Do not grade plans for an issue you have not claimed; a plan comment
upstream is a public commitment.

## Rules for working in other people's repos

The readers are strangers, and four rules apply:

- **A posted plan is a claim with an approach attached.** If someone
  else has already posted a concrete plan on the issue, do not post a
  competing one; engage theirs in the thread, or pick again. Parallel
  plans in a shared space are a race.
- **Maintainer direction outranks your plan.** If the thread already
  says how the maintainers want this handled, your plan either follows
  that direction or opens a conversation about it before you build.
  Building against stated direction wastes your work and their
  patience.
- **Branch conventions are the repo's.** A `fix/<issue>-<slug>` habit is
  a good default, but the repo's contributing docs (the ones you noted
  in your repo-local `CLAUDE.local.md`) decide branch, commit, and PR
  conventions. The repo's stated asks always win.
- **Deviations get reported upstream, not just recorded.** Updating
  your plan is not enough: if your approach changes materially after you
  posted the plan comment, say so in the thread before the pull request
  appears. A maintainer who read plan A should never be surprised by
  diff B.
