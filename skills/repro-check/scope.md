# Scope: where your issue lives

Like every scope file, this one says where the skill works: the rubric
(`rubric.md`) decides whether a package or a claim draft is READY; the
scope decides which issues the skill may work against at all, and what
the rules are where that issue lives.

## Where your issue lives

Any public repository, one issue at a time: the issue your claim
names. That issue comes to you through your own evidence trail: an
issue graded by issue-scout or issue-select (a direct pick, or a scan's
top results). Do not grade drafts or packages for an issue you never
graded.

## Rules for working in other people's repos

The readers are strangers who owe you nothing, and three rules apply:

- **A stranger's claim blocks you.** A claim comment or an assignee
  on an issue means someone got there first, and you have no channel
  to coordinate with them. Never claim over one; pick again. First
  claim stands, including yours: if a stranger claims after you, your
  claim holds, and if you stop working, release your claim honestly
  in the thread.
- **Never piggyback, and never duplicate.** If the thread already
  holds a reproduction, do not post "same here, can confirm". Add a
  comment only when it carries something new from your environment,
  in your words.
- **The repo's own rules outrank yours.** CONTRIBUTING.md and any
  stated contribution or AI policy are the standard you are held to
  whether you read them or not. When a stated ask conflicts with one of
  your habits, the repo wins; say so in your evidence if it changes
  what you do.
